01 — RESEARCH

Transition-aware CFD

My research combines transition modelling and scale-resolving simulation to investigate separated flows where conventional fully turbulent assumptions can miss important boundary-layer physics.

01 / MODEL

BCM-DES

A transition-aware hybrid BCM-DES formulation combining the SA-BCM transition model with the SA-DDES framework through a blending parameter, Φ = fd × γBC.

02 / SOLVER

PRAVAHA-IITH

Implementation and testing were carried out using the PRAVAHA-IITH compressible, unstructured finite-volume solver developed at IIT Hyderabad.

03 / VALIDATION

Transition Validation

The BCM-DES formulation was validated using Schubauer-Klebanoff, T3A and T3B flat-plate transition benchmarks before application to separated aerodynamic flows.

02 — MODEL IMPLEMENTATION

One framework. Two capabilities.

BCM-DES was developed to combine transition detection with the scale-resolving capability of a delayed detached-eddy simulation framework.

The formulation modifies the DDES length scale using the transition-aware blending parameter, without introducing additional transport equations.

03 — CASE STUDIES

Three flows. Three transition problems.

The thesis applies BCM-DES to stationary and rotating cylinders and to low-Reynolds-number airfoil flow, examining how transition changes separation, drag and lift.

CASE 01 / BLUFF BODY

Stationary Cylinder

Investigation of the drag crisis across Reynolds numbers from 5×10⁴ to 10⁶, including pressure recovery, separation behaviour and three-dimensional wake structures.

CASE 02 / ROTATING FLOW

Rotating Cylinder

Study of rotation-induced asymmetric transition at Re = 152,000 across spin ratios from α = 0 to 1.0.

CASE 03 / AIRFOIL

Eppler 387

Low-Reynolds-number airfoil study focused on laminar separation bubble formation, bubble migration, bursting and static stall at Re = 100,000.

06 — CONTRIBUTIONS

Research contributions

Key technical contributions identified in the thesis.

Transition-aware DES formulation

BCM-DES uses Φ = fd × γBC as the blending factor to couple transition modelling with the DDES framework, with no additional transport equations required.

Drag crisis prediction

BCM-DES captured the transition from laminar to turbulent separation and the associated reduction in cylinder drag across Re = 5×10⁴ to 10⁶, using meshes of at most ~2.5 million cells versus 38–100 million for wall-resolved LES.

Lift crisis prediction

At Re = 152,000 and α = 0.25, BCM-DES predicted the negative lift associated with the lift-crisis regime (Cl = −0.0185), including the correct sign — unlike standard SA-DDES, which predicted a qualitatively wrong positive lift.

Low-Reynolds-number airfoil flow

The Eppler 387 simulations captured laminar separation-bubble behaviour and its migration toward the leading edge near stall, with stall onset near α ≈ 11°–12° at Re = 100,000.
